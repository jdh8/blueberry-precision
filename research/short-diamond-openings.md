# A one-diamond minimum for Blueberry Precision

Research date: 2026-09-25. Shapes are in ♠–♥–♦–♣ order.

## Closest documented precedent: Groetheim–Tundal

The exact combination being considered has international tournament precedent. In Phillip Alder's report of Brazil–Norway at the 2009 Bermuda Bowl, the WBF's own bulletin describes Glenn Groetheim and Ulf Tundal's strong-club system as follows:

| Opening | Meaning reported in the bulletin |
| --- | --- |
| 1♦ | At least one diamond; 11–15 points, sometimes 16 |
| 2♣ | 11–15 points, at least six clubs, with precisely 4=4=0=5 as the exception |

The 2♣ explanation accompanies Board 4 on printed page 12; the 1♦ explanation accompanies Board 7 on printed page 13. This establishes an actual played partnership method, rather than merely a suggested system modification. The bulletin does not provide their complete uncontested 2♣ continuations. [WBF, São Paulo 2009 Bulletin 2, pp. 11–13](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/09_2%20SaoPaulo/pdf/Bul_02.pdf).

### Continuations: what the sources establish

Their 2014–15 convention card lists 2♦ as an artificial inquiry forcing one
round, and direct 2♥/2♠ responses as nonforcing with five or more cards. It says
further relays are possible after 2♣–2♦, without specifying the shape responses.
The card was available through indexed text during this research; the original
PDF download returned 404. [Grøtheim–Tundal convention card, p. 2](https://www.bridge.no/var/fckeditor/bridgeno/file/PDF/SM/2014-15/Systemkort/MT1_Groetheim-Tundal.pdf)

A played auction confirms 2♣–2♦–2♠ showing spades: they continued to 4♠ in the
2007 Bermuda Bowl final. It does not identify their treatment of 4405.
[WBF 2007 final report](https://db.worldbridge.org/Repository/tourn/shanghai.07/Bulletin/bul1505.htm)

The detailed 2003 Aa–Grøtheim supplementary notes, notes 6 and 10, belong to a
different version: 2♣ requires six clubs and 1M can contain four cards with
five clubs. Those notes cannot establish the later 4405 exception's relay path.
[Aa–Grøtheim 2003, PDF pp. 2, 12–13](https://stara.pzbs.pl/wyniki/2003/zagranica/bermuda_bowl/karty_konw/aa-groeheim.pdf)

The precise Grøtheim–Tundal rebid showing 4405 remains unverified. Barronen's
explicit 2♣–2♦–3NT = 4405 below is a separate treatment, not evidence of their
agreement.

## Minimal application to this system

With the user's stated preference to retain 1♦ on 4=4=1=4, the relevant candidate is:

- **1♦: 10–15 HCP, 1+♦**, otherwise retaining its existing catch-all role.
- **2♣: 10–15 HCP, 6+♣ or precisely 4=4=0=5.**

This is a proposed adaptation, not an implemented change. Its shape logic is independent of the historical system: when neither major has five cards and clubs have at most five, a diamond void forces 4+4+5 in the other suits. Therefore 4=4=0=5 is the only additional shape that needs to leave 1♦ to remove the diamond-void possibility. Six-card club hands already have 2♣ available. The singleton shapes 4=4=1=4, 4=3=1=5 and 3=4=1=5 can remain in 1♦.

The implication for 2♣ responses is that clubs are no longer unconditionally six cards. The partnership needs to identify the exceptional shape, or at least find either four-card major, without relying on a six-card club fit. A dedicated shape route can be designed within the current inquiry structure; the historical opening definitions alone do not supply that continuation.

## Broader five-card-club approaches

Jay Barronen's first-person account of his Finntunes treatment recommends opening 2♣ with six clubs or with a five-card club suit and shortness, while sending 5♣332 and 5♣422 hands through 1♦/1NT. His published relay responses explicitly assign **2♣–2♦–3NT to 4=4=0=5**; additional relays describe shapes such as 3=4=1=5. This demonstrates that five clubs with diamond shortness is a workable system-design direction. It is broader than the minimal exception above, and its 3NT rebid should not be transplanted into Blueberry's inquiry without checking the forcing level and all existing branches. [Barronen, “Weaknesses and Strengths of Precision 2♣ Opening,” 2013-10-21](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/weaknesses-and-strengths-of-precision-2c-opening/).

Two older discussions independently describe the same general routing idea, though these are practitioner proposals rather than evidence of a particular established partnership:

- A 2009 proposal explicitly keeps 4=4=1=4 in 1♦ and opens 4=4=0=5 with 2♣, preserving Multi and other weak two-level openings. [ctj, post 1642](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/59/puzzles-other-games/bridge-96072/index66.html).
- A 2014 proposed variant sends 4=4=0=5 and both (43)15 shapes to 2♣ and treats 4=4=1=4 as balanced for opening purposes. [whereagles, posts 1 and 3](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/66007-precision-1d2c-variant-questions/).

## Why a two-diamond minimum is a different change

Daniel Neill's *Standard Modern Precision*, second edition, offers the familiar alternative: 1♦ can have two diamonds, 2♣ requires six clubs, and 2♦ contains exactly 4=4=1=4, 4=4=0=5, 4=3=1=5 and 3=4=1=5. Thus a 2+♦ system need not open five-card clubs with 2♣ at all; it can reserve another opening for the short-diamond shapes. [Author's opening table, printed p. 12, publisher sample](https://ebooksbridge.com/uploads/files/products/samples/705/SAMPLE_HONORS_StandardModernPrecision.pdf).

For Blueberry, moving five-card-club hands alone cannot create a 2+♦ guarantee while 4=4=1=4 still opens 1♦. Since the user explicitly accepts that singleton, the one-diamond minimum and the narrow 4=4=0=5 exception are the relevant scope.

## WBF and EBL: does 1+ exclude unusual defenses?

The relevant rules concern Brown Sticker overcalls, not simply whether an
opening is alerted. WBF's current linked policy is the 2024 edition; EBL's live
Systems page links to its April 2026 edition. Their wording differs.

- **WBF:** Section 2.5(b) restricts overcalls without four cards in a known suit
  over natural one-level suit openings, subject to stated exceptions. Note II
  extends this to an ostensibly natural, nonforcing minor opening even when it
  can contain a singleton or doubleton. Once the opening is ostensibly natural
  and nonforcing, the restriction expressly applies; a singleton possibility
  does not disqualify it, and a two-card minimum is unnecessary.
  [WBF Systems Policy 2024, p. 3](https://www.worldbridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WBFSystemsPolicy.pdf#page=3)
- **EBL:** Section 2.4(b) contains the corresponding overcall restriction.
  Section 2.4(f) specifically covers ostensibly natural, nonforcing short **club**
  openings; section 2.4(g) covers the other minor in a strong-minor system when
  it is natural or balanced without a five-card major. It does not expressly
  include an unbalanced singleton-diamond 4=4=1=4 hand in that latter safe
  harbor. [EBL Systems Policy April 2026, p. 3](https://championships.eurobridge.org/Doc/EBL_Systems_Policy.pdf#page=3)

**Assessment:** moving 4=4=0=5 establishes the one-diamond guarantee. The sources
above do not establish a blanket ruling that this alone removes all additional
defensive options against Blueberry's catchall. The unresolved question is how
the event classifies the complete 1♦ definition, including the retained
singleton shapes. A documented international precedent establishes playability,
not a binding classification for this system.
