# Strong 1♥ rebids after a negative 1♦ response to strong 1♣

Research date: 2026-10-25. Scope: uncontested **1♣–1♦–1♥**, where
1♣ is a strong opening, 1♦ is negative, and opener's 1♥ rebid includes
extra strength without necessarily showing hearts. Both a purely strong
artificial rebid and a mixture of strong hands with minimum natural hearts
qualify. Ranges below describe the cited versions, usually with an unpassed
responder; they should not be transferred between versions.

## TOSR: a complete pure-strong structure

The first-party PDF identifies **Joon Pahk and Leo Zelevinsky**, version 1.0.6,
5 November 2008. Its 1♣ opening is 16+ unbalanced or 17+ balanced. The negative
is precisely 0–7 HCP with 0–2 controls, **or any strength with 0–1 controls**;
any three-control hand responds positively. Controls count ace = 2, king = 1.

After the strong 1♥ rebid, 1♠ is a second negative, 0–5 HCP; 1NT and higher
show semi-positives, normally 6+ HCP, using the positive shape structure shifted
up two steps. After **1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠**, opener's 1NT shows 20–22 balanced and
2♣ shows 23+ any shape or slightly less with exceptional playing strength.
Minimum natural hearts instead rebid **1♣–1♦–2♥**, 16–19 with 5+ hearts.
This makes TOSR a close, fully documented analogue to the approach in this repo.
[TOSR, pp. 2–5 and 7](https://www.bantha.org/~pahk/bridge/tosr/TOSR_1c.pdf#page=4).

## Symmetric Relay 1986: an earlier documented example

The notes bear the names **Hugh Grosvenor and Ian Robinson**. The section
“Continuations After the 1D Negative” explicitly says some negative responders
intend to force to game because their hands lack two controls. This matters if
“negative” is intended to mean an unconditional HCP ceiling.

After artificial strong 1♥, responder's 1♠ is a second negative, approximately
0–4, possibly 5 balanced. A semi-positive uses the normal shape responses
shifted up two steps and is forcing on responder to game. Following
**1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠**, opener's 1NT is 20–22, 2♣ establishes a game force, and
2NT is 23–24. Below the strong threshold, natural hearts rebid 2♥, showing
5+ hearts unbalanced and 16 to a poor 19. The opening itself is 16+ unbalanced
or 17+ balanced. The available source is a mirror of the authored notes,
rather than an identified author-maintained original.
[Symmetric Relay 1986](https://www.geocities.ws/oldyank1/precision-relay.html).

## Tarzan 2023: a clean 0–8 negative and 18+ artificial rebid

W “Tom” Reynolds describes his own partnership's move to a **15+ forcing 1♣**
and expressly gives **1♣–1♦ (0–8 HCP)–1♥ (artificial 18+ HCP)**. Responses
from 1NT upward use a modified symmetric structure derived from Sam Dinkin's
Infernal Machine. The article is dated 20 April 2023; it is direct evidence
from a practitioner, although it is not a complete uncontested system manual.

Use the version name deliberately: the author distinguishes his contemporary
methods from older Tarzan and symmetric-relay implementations. This source
supports the requested basic auction without the high-HCP negative exceptions
in TOSR and the 1986 notes.
[Reynolds, “Forcing Club Theory and the Tarzan Precision 1C”](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/forcing-club-theory-and-the-tarzan-precision-1c/).

## Olausson: an especially clear explanation of the NT ladder

Stefan Olausson's 12 September 2020 first-person article describes a 16+
strong-club partnership. It calls 1♥ an artificial force, **usually** showing
18+ or so, and responder normally continues with artificial 1♠. Its balanced
routes are:

| Auction | Strength |
|---|---|
| 1♣–1♦–1NT | 16–17 |
| 1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠–1NT | 18–19 |
| 1♣–1♦–2NT | 20–21 |
| 1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠–2NT | 22–23 |
| 1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠–2♣–2♦–2NT | 24–25 |
| Same start through 2♣–2♦, then 2♥–2♠–2NT | 26+ |

The final 2♥ is Kokish. Thus this is evidence for **strong artificial 1♥**,
but not for “all 18+ hands must rebid 1♥”: 20–21 balanced bids 2NT directly.
The article does not specify the original negative's numerical limit or fully
define any exceptions to “usually.”
[Olausson, “NT-intervals after a strong-1♣ and neg-1♦”](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/nt-intervals-after-a-strong-1c-and-neg-1d/).

## New Precision: strong hands of any shape mixed with minimum hearts

The online chapter of **《新精确叫牌法（中级版）》** defines 1♣ as all 16+
hands and 1♦ as a 0–7-point negative. Read the detailed sections together:
printed p. 16 routes **16–18 with hearts** through the same puppet 1♥ used
by **19+ any shape**; opener later returns to 2♥ with 16–18 and 5+ hearts.
After **1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠** (0–4), printed p. 17 gives 1NT as 19–21 balanced,
nonforcing; 2♣ repeats the inquiry with 22+; 2♥ identifies the minimum heart
hand and is nonforcing.
[Chapter 2, printed pp. 15–17, PDF pp. 1–3](https://mybridge.club/zb_users/upload/2022/01/202201271643267618446868.pdf#page=2).

The system's 2019 Q&A independently addresses the exact ambiguity: responder
must leave enough room for opener's 16–18 heart hand. It revises responder's
rebids so none exceeds 2♥: **1NT shows 5–7 with 5–5+ minors or a 6+ minor**,
replacing higher minor-showing responses. This is useful evidence of the
practical design problem, as well as confirmation that the weak heart branch
is intentional.
[新精确微刊，第7期, “有问必答,” 1 July 2019](https://mybridge.club/?id=108).

## Caroline Club: a defined Kokish-style mixture

The author-maintained notes credit original developers **Scott Benson and
Doug Bone**, with a revision dated 9 April 2026. First/second-seat 1♣ contains
all 16+ hands. In “The 1♣=1♦ Sequence,” 1♥ is explicitly Kokish: **hearts,
19–20 balanced, 23–24 balanced, or 27+ balanced**. Responder's 1♠ is normally
forced, with terrible long-suit hands allowed to break the relay.

After 1♠, opener's 1NT shows 19–20 balanced; 2♣/2♦ show hearts plus that
minor; 2♥ shows a minimum with 6+ hearts; 2♠ shows hearts and spades with
extras; 2NT shows 23–24 balanced. Consequently, this preserves minimum
natural-heart hands inside 1♥, while the extra-strength alternatives are
specific balanced ranges. It is less general than New Precision's “19+ any
shape.” Separate direct rebids cater to other strong hands.
[The Caroline Club, “The One Club Opening” and “The 1♣=1♦ Sequence”](https://floyd.verio.com/bridge/caroclub/caroline.htm).

## Existing Viking reference and a further Moscito example

The repo already cites a bidding tree labelled **Viking Precision 2007**. A
fresh retrieval confirms **1♣ 16+ any, 1♦ 0–7 any, and 1♥ relay 20+ any**.
Its displayed continuations differ from the repo's current tables: after
1♥–1♠ it lists 1NT as 20–21 and 2♣ as 22+, rather than 20–22 and 23+.
It therefore supports the basic precedent, but should not be cited as an exact
transcription of every continuation in `src/1C/1D.md`. The tree does not identify
authorship; the independently authored sources above provide firmer provenance.
[Viking Precision 2007 bidding tree](https://bridge.downagain.be/FD/main.php?vul=9&dealer=9&system=smp7cltvhrh).

**Terrorist's Moscito**, Bo-Yin Yang and contributing authors, 28 March 2000,
also uses this architecture. Its 1♣ is 10+ relay points; 1♦ is negative,
0–4 RP, possibly 5 RP balanced. All strong hands, approximately 18+ in the
notes' “value” terminology, continue with the artificial 1♥ second relay.
Responder's 1♠ is 0–2 RP (about 0–5 HCP); higher calls are semi-positive.
This is an additional first-party precedent, but its evaluation units should
not be mistaken for an 18-HCP guarantee. The notes explain their separate
methods for the minimum heart hands displaced by artificial 1♥.
[Terrorist's Moscito, §§6.1–6.3, printed pp. 53–55 / PDF pp. 55–57](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/terr_mosc.pdf#page=55).

## Interpretation

For a pure strong rebid close to the repo's current design, **TOSR** supplies
the most directly reusable full continuations; **Tarzan 2023** supplies a clear
HCP-limited negative. For a strong rebid retaining minimum hearts, **New
Precision** is the closest literal match, while **Caroline Club** documents a
precise Kokish alternative. This is a comparison of the recovered agreements,
not evidence that one treatment plays better.

Kit Woolsey's **“Long Relay”** inspired Olausson's article but is not itself a
match: Woolsey describes a negative-response structure using artificial **2♥**
and direct 2NT. The article's 1♥ response shows a positive hand with spades;
it is not opener's 1♥ rebid after negative 1♦.
[Woolsey, “Long Relay,” 6 September 2020](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/long-relay/).
