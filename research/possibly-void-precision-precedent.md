# Possibly-void Precision 1♦: international defensive classification

Research date: 2026-09-27. The question is whether 1♦ was treated as natural
**for Brown Sticker restrictions on the opponents' overcalls**. The opening's
own legality, its system's BLUE category, and the convention-card artificial
checkbox do not answer that question.

## Shanghai 2007: relevant report, incomplete official record

On 23 August 2007, Jan Martel reported receiving that day's Shanghai systems
update. She reproduced a statement attributed to the Systems Committee
chairman: nonforcing one-minor openings were intended to be treated as natural;
an opening with a strong option could permit overcalls otherwise classed as
Brown Sticker. Martel expressly applied this to Precision 1♦. Her later post
identified John Wignall as the chairman making the Shanghai rulings. This is a
contemporaneous first-person report of receipt of an official notice, but the
original notice has not been recovered. [Martel, post 79](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/20698-shanghai-brown-sticker-bids/page__st__60),
[follow-up discussion, posts 88 and 94](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/20698-shanghai-brown-sticker-bids/page__st__80).

The reported formulation imposes no diamond-length minimum. Extending it to a
nonforcing 0+ Precision 1♦ is therefore a reasonable reading of the reported
wording, **not a recovered void-specific official judgment**. Subsequent forum
discussion also raises the possibility of later changes to the treatment of
short clubs; that must be resolved before describing the August statement as
the final policy actually enforced throughout the event.

The void interpretation is contemporaneous, rather than invented in this
research: post 103 explicitly discusses treating 0+ diamond openings as
natural. It is a participant's reaction, not an official ruling. Later, on
5 October, post 109 says short 2+ clubs were officially artificial. It gives
no supporting ruling and does not discuss void diamonds, so it does not
establish a reversal for 0+ Precision 1♦. [Shanghai discussion, posts 103
and 109](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/20698-shanghai-brown-sticker-bids/page__st__100).

The official first bulletin expressly prohibited Brown Sticker conventions in
the round robin and throughout the Senior Bowl. Thus Shanghai included the
kind of restricted event/stage asked about. [WBF Shanghai 2007 Bulletin 1,
printed p. 6](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Docs/Bulletins/2007/2007BermudaBowl-Shanghai/bul_01.pdf).

Bulletin 6 announced a revised Brink–Drijver card that included Brown Sticker
conventions and was approved **for the knockout stage**. That announcement
alone does not establish any reversal of short-minor classification: allowing
Brown Sticker overcalls at that stage is compatible with classifying the
opposing opening as natural. [WBF Shanghai 2007 Bulletin 6, printed p.
20](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Docs/Bulletins/2007/2007BermudaBowl-Shanghai/bul_06.pdf).

All fifteen Shanghai 2007 bulletins in the ECats archive were searched for
Brown Sticker references and the minor-opening clarification. No official
reproduction of the clarification or void-specific defensive ruling was
located.

## Current policy does not close the void gap

WBF's 2024 policy, §2.5(b), Note II, expressly extends the overcall restriction
to ostensibly natural nonforcing minor openings that can contain a singleton
or doubleton. It does not expressly mention a void. That omission is not
itself a ruling that void-capable openings are artificial, but it prevents
using this text alone as an explicit guarantee of their natural status.
[WBF Systems Policy 2024, p. 3](https://www.worldbridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WBFSystemsPolicy.pdf#page=3).

EBL's April 2026 policy, §2.4(g), covers the other minor in a strong-minor
system when that opening is natural or balanced without a five-card major.
It does not explicitly protect a catchall containing unbalanced hands with
zero diamonds. Thus the 2007 report cannot simply be treated as a current,
universal answer under both organisations' policies.
[EBL Systems Policy April 2026, p. 3](https://championships.eurobridge.org/Doc/EBL_Systems_Policy.pdf#page=3).

## Discarded lead: Sanremo 2009 Appeal 20

The EBL's official appeal report expressly describes Franzel–Kriftner's
Precision 1♦ as possibly containing zero diamonds. Opponents Pachtman–Ginossar
disagreed about whether a later 1NT intervention showed the minors or the other
two suits. The director and committee treated this as misinformation about
their actual agreement. The decision does **not** classify 1♦ as natural for
Brown Sticker purposes. Both described defenses also show known suits, so
their availability does not settle the question. [EBL Sanremo 2009 Appeals,
Appeal 20, PDF pp. 40–41](https://www.eurobridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/2009-Appeals-Sanremo.pdf#page=40).

## Evidence limit

The material recovered in this search supports a qualified report of a 2007
announcement protecting nonforcing Precision 1♦. It does not yet establish a
final, explicit international ruling about a **possibly-void** version of that
opening. Permission to use that opening, BLUE classification, or an artificial
checkbox on a filed card should not be substituted for that missing evidence.
