# Minor-opening ranges with a 14–16 1NT

Research date: 2026-09-30. These are documented versions, not a claim that each
partnership still plays the same methods today.

## Documented precedents

| System/version | 1♦ | 2♣ | 1NT | Strong 1♣ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meckstroth–Rodwell, 2016 USBC | 11–15, normally 2+♦ | 11–15, 6+♣ | 14–16; 15–17 in third seat vulnerable and fourth seat | Summary says 16+ |
| Diamond–Platnick, 2016 WBF card | 11–15, normally 2+♦ | 11–15, 6+♣ | 14–16; 15–17 in third seat vulnerable and fourth seat | Card says 16+; ordinary balanced rebid after the negative starts at 17 |
| Daniel Neill, *Standard Modern Precision*, second edition (2017) | Good 10–15 unbalanced, or 11–13 balanced; 2+♦ | Good 10–15, 6+♣ | 14–16 | 16+ unbalanced, 17+ balanced |

Sources and qualifications:

- **Meckstroth–Rodwell:** The partnership's filed summary explicitly permits
  opening below 11 when justified, as well as upgrading a suitable 13 into
  1NT. Third-seat openings can be lighter and shorter. Thus its 11–15 label
  is a default evaluated range rather than an absolute raw-HCP boundary.
  [USBF 2016 system summary, updated 2016-04-09](https://usbf.org/apps/teamforms.php?fid=1438&pid=210&task=display).
- **Diamond–Platnick:** The card permits a singleton diamond honor; the 2♣
  opening can have five cards or be light in third seat. It also explicitly
  notes frequent upgrades and discretion about HCP. After 1♣–1♦, 1NT is
  17–18; its direct 2NT is normally 19–21. The printed 16+ label for 1♣
  should therefore not be read as requiring all balanced 16-counts to open
  1♣ in seats where 1NT is 14–16.
  [WBF convention card, pp. 1–3](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/systems/2016WorldChampionships-Wroclaw/OpenTeams/USA/Diamond-Platnick.pdf).
- **Standard Modern Precision:** The opening table explicitly separates
  good-10 unbalanced hands from 11–13 balanced hands. Its 1NT includes
  5M332 and 5m422. The following discussion allows upgrading a playing-strength
  15 into 1♣. The system therefore combines aggressive lower limits with a
  nominal 15 ceiling outside notrump, while retaining a 17+ balanced club.
  [Publisher's author sample, printed pp. 12–14](https://masterpointpress.com/uploads/files/products/samples/705/SAMPLE_HONORS_StandardModernPrecision.pdf#page=18).

## A useful selective 16-count exception

Dan Neill's 2019 compilation *Greco–Hampson Precision* describes 1♦ and 2♣ as
10–15, 1♣ as 16+ unbalanced or 17+ balanced, and an ordinary 14–16 1NT
range with seat/style adjustments. They specifically allow **1♦ with a
16-count three-suiter or 5431 hand containing four or five diamonds**, on
the stated ground that these hands fare better in competition outside 1♣.
The notes also allow decent balanced 10-counts nonvulnerable.

This is evidence for a selected downgrade, not an across-the-board 16-point
ceiling on both minor openings. It is also distinct from the stricter
11-point balanced minimum in Neill's published SMP opening table.
[Greco–Hampson notes, opening summary, p. 2](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Greco-Hampson-Precision_Part1.pdf).
Unlike the filed partnership cards above, this is Neill's account of another
partnership's methods, not a verified partnership-authored primary source.
Evidence limitation: the opening summary was available in the search index,
but fetching the original PDF returned 404 during this research. Its indexed
1NT seat descriptions also contain an apparent repeated-vulnerability typo;
they should not be used to establish its exact seat schedule.

## Interpretation

A documented alternative with a nominal 16 ceiling is the *Modified Precision
System with Positive Transfer Responses* filed on the Canadian Bridge
Federation site. Its opening table uses 14–16 1NT, generally 17+ 1♣ (with
playing-strength exceptions), 12–16 1♦ plus selected 11-counts with four or
five diamonds, and 10–16 2♣. This establishes that the higher ceiling is
playable as part of a different strong-club boundary; it is not an exact
11–16-both-minors precedent.
[CBF system notes, opening table, PDF p. 2](https://cbf.ca/BWeek/26files/CCards/B3-LEVESQUE_Level2_Modified-Precision-System.pdf#page=2).

The sources support **11–15 as a conventional default**, with good-10
openings an established aggressive alternative. They do not make 11–16
necessary simply because 1NT includes 16: balanced 16 belongs to 1NT;
unbalanced 16 normally belongs to 1♣. Raising the lower minor-opening limit
and raising its upper limit are separate choices.

A blanket 11–16 minor range would intentionally move unbalanced 16-counts
out of 1♣ and require corresponding changes to the strong-club definition
and continuation ranges. Selectively downgrading an unsuitable 16-count is
a smaller adjustment, and the Greco–Hampson example documents that approach.
These are system-design inferences from the opening definitions, not measured
claims that one range wins more often.
